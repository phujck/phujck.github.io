4. What A1 actually did — the packed-constant finding

A1 closed 100 of 100 items: every one of its formulas, translated faithfully and executed, reproduces its sequence byte-for-byte. Its φ is nonetheless 0.017182, because 97 of those 100 answers are one template:

a(n) = floor(K / 2^n) mod 2,  n = 0..11

K is the item itself, packed as an integer and read least-significant-bit first. S-1 — the base-encoded-literal rule, preregistered in s1_rule.md §1 and active from the first classification — bins those 97 as PRINTS (×1/100), not abstraction. The three survivors are the ones S-1's length gate spares.

This is the exact template s1_rule.md §4.3 documents for gemini_2.5_pro on the released program sheet, where 13 rows of packed constants were hand-labelled as non-copies and inflated that model's φ by 11×. The bare arm rediscovered the same trick unprompted, and the same rule catches it.

The freeze forbids showing the alternative. §3.4 is explicit: S-1 runs on the first classification, with "no rebin step, no before/after column, no opportunity to see a φ under one instrument and then choose the other". So this report carries no no-S-1 column for A1 — even though S-1 is by far the largest single instrument effect in the campaign and it lands hardest on our own baseline. What is reported instead is the count — 97 fires on A1, 3 on A2, 0 on A3 — with every fired row named in report.json.

Read A1 accordingly. Its φ is a statement about the form of a one-shot answer, not about whether the model can hit the sequence: on correctness alone A1 is 100/100. DEV-1 compounds this — A1 is one shot where the published rows had a five-point temperature sweep, so A1 is a lower bound on a published row, not a replication of one.

DEV-5 cross-check on our own arms

A2 and A3 bank a program, and the program decides their correctness. Their formula text is independently translated and executed as a cross-check that decides nothing — it can only expose a formula whose text says something other than what its program does.

No disagreement is a formula/program mismatch. Every one was read: they are notations the translator declines — a prose function (leading decimal digit of n!), congruence notation (n^2 = 4 (mod 10)), a two-clause piecewise table-then-recurrence, a recurrence with a separate parity readout, and a three-branch chained conditional. In no case does an arm's formula text assert something its program does not do. That is the result the cross-check exists to produce, and it is a null one.

The untranslatable remainder is a scrutiny gap, not a correctness gap: those formulas state their rule in mathematical prose (Legendre symbols, Ulam numbers, prime(n), morphism fixed points) that the translator will not render, because rendering it would mean the scorer writing mathematics the arm did not write. The translator is complete exactly where it decides correctness — 100/100 on A1 and 4800/4800 on the published formula sheets — and incomplete only where it is a second opinion on our own work.
