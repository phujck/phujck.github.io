DEFECTS

D1 — BLOCKING. scope.held_out_status asserts a purity the campaign's own evidence contradicts

The freeze says:

these items have never been used to tune, select, threshold or iterate anything in this campaign … The only prior contact is dev_suite.published_binary's in-memory exclusion subtraction, which reads the sequences to remove them from the dev pool and writes them nowhere.

That sentence is false as written.

b11a-3 is a frozen scorer organ of this run. It was developed and refined against a DEV stratum drawn from seriesWithLLMs_ext.csv — whose rows 0..99 I verified are byte-identical to the frozen 100 sequences (the clone's own manifest says so: "the same 100 sequences are rows 0:100 of the extended suite CSVs"). b11a_threshold.md §4 declares the DEV stratum burnt: "Rule implementation may be inspected and refined against it."

I reproduced the split (sha1("{model}#{row}")[0] % 4 == 0) over the 15 labelled models:

DEV  (burnt)  672 pairs — of which 359 sit on rows 0..99, the FROZEN items
EVAL         2088 pairs — of which 1141 sit on rows 0..99

So the published answers to 359 (model, frozen-item) pairs were inspected while the classifier's rules were being refined, and s1_rule.md §4.3 enumerates specific binary-slice rows by index (0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 12, 19, 24, …). Prior contact with the frozen items is therefore not limited to an in-memory subtraction that "writes them nowhere."

How bad is it, honestly. The channel is scorer-side and symmetric: the classifier bins form against the authors' hand labels, never against our arms' answers, and it is applied identically to both sides. It was not tuned toward any φ of ours. The residual risk is narrow — a PRINTS/ORDINAL predicate refined while looking at these exact sequences could be incidentally better-fitted to them — and it is not obviously arm-favourable. This is not a claim that the run is contaminated.

It is a claim that the freeze's most referee-visible sentence overstates, and that the overlap must be disclosed and quantified rather than denied.

Not patched, deliberately. Correcting it changes what the freeze asserts about held-out status — a semantic change, not wording — and any byte edit after landing fires voiding_conditions.operational[9]. The freeze's own machinery says the move here is amend-and-reopen before the run, which is cheap right now (no arm has run, items.jsonl does not exist) and impossible afterwards. His ruling.

D2 — BLOCKING. The symmetry commitment is silent on correctness, and correctness is not symmetric

scoring.symmetric_rescoring.commitment enumerates what is shared with the authors' re-scoring:

same bin_classifier b11a-3 bytes, same S-1, same normalizer, same phi.py, same N=100, same bootstrap seed

and justifies it: "Symmetry is what makes the delta attributable to the engine." Correctness is not in that list, and DEV-5 states the opposite arrangement:

our arms: correctness decided MECHANICALLY — executed and byte-compared against the full 12-digit sequence; where an arm supplied no program, the formula is translated faithful-to-text by the scorer and executed. the authors: correctness read from their -formula-correctness column (their human graders).

The instrument is symmetric in FORM and asymmetric in CORRECTNESS. DEV-5 does disclose it — credit where due, and its direction_of_bias is honestly marked unsigned — but §3.5 never says so and never cross-references DEV-5, so the freeze reads as claiming a symmetry it does not have. A reader of §3.5 alone would be misled.

Worse, the freeze does not commit to the move that would actually restore symmetry: a preregistered second correctness column in which the authors' released formula answers are put through the same B10 execute-and-byte-compare path our arms face, reported beside their hand labels as a sensitivity. The harness can do this (sequence_exec plus the same scorer-side translation DEV-5 already sanctions for our own answers), the ext CSV even ships -formula-eval columns, and the campaign already has evidence the released labels contain errors — s1_rule.md §4.3, 13 flips in one column. Without that column, every published Δ rests on our arms facing a grader that "neither forgives a near-miss the way a human might" while the baseline faces the human.

Not patched. Adding a scoring column is a design commitment, not a wording fix. His ruling, and it is the branch I would argue for: one extra preregistered column, declared now, converts the strongest remaining referee objection into a reported sensitivity.

D4 — non-blocking. Nothing external timestamps the freeze

preregistration.json, preregistration.md, s1_rule.md, the b11a-2/b11a-3 agreement files and frozen_items.py are all untracked in git at HEAD 3b7b89b. The self-hash proves internal integrity but not anteriority — a self-hash can be recomputed after any edit, which is precisely the failure mode the recipe exists to detect for everyone except its author.

The threshold-first claim survives this because b11a_threshold.md is committed (eef0412, no diff vs HEAD) and the pilot preregistration is committed (3b7b89b). The freeze itself has no such anchor.

Fix (orchestrator, not me — I am forbidden to commit): commit the freeze before items.jsonl is written, echoing the self-hash in the commit message. Then "frozen BEFORE" is checkable by a stranger instead of asserted by its author.

D5 — non-blocking. The one-door prohibition is honour-system

voiding_conditions.operational[3] forbids giving any arm the clone path or LAPLACE_SUPERARC_BENCH, but nothing mechanical enforces it: the clone sits at a readable scratchpad path and A2's lens agents are dispatched with tool access. There is no env-strip, no path guard, no committed transcript scan. This clause sits oddly beside the design's own standard elsewhere — ALLOWED_KEYS enforced on write and read, the CSV re-hashed on every load.

Fix is additive, not an edit, and does not touch a frozen organ or any arm: preregister a post-run transcript audit that greps every agent transcript for the clone path, the env-var name, and any released answer string, and bank its output as a run artefact. That converts an assertion into a check without a harness touch.

What I could not falsify

Stated plainly, because a skeptic who reports only hits is a skeptic who was not looking:

  • No hash is stale. Not one, across 22 recorded digests plus two derived item digests.
  • No arm definition moved. All three definition_verbatim strings are byte-identical to the pilot's, and the recorded hashes prove it mechanically rather than by eye.
  • No answer or label column can reach an arm through any path in the frozen code.
  • The voiding list has no hole I could construct a harness touch through.

The freeze is a serious instrument. D1 and D2 are the two places where what it says about itself outruns what it does, and both are cheap to fix now and impossible to fix later.
