EigenEngine

Eigen is a routing and permission system for AI agents, the programs that answer questions and carry out tasks over an organisation's documents. It keeps the documents as a graph, routes each question to the few passages it needs, enforces who may read what, and returns every answer with its sources. Pre-release. eigen@gmccaul.co.uk

The problem

What goes wrong with agents today

Context

An agent starts every question from nothing. It reads the whole pile, or whatever someone pasted in, and the pile grows. In a chain of agents, one wrong source at the first step is carried into every step after it and is invisible by the end.

Governance

To find anything, an agent has to read everything. A question about a supplier reads the personnel files and the board minutes too, because there is no way to know a file is irrelevant, or private, without opening it. Nothing records which files were read, so the answer cannot be checked against them, by the user or by an auditor.

How Eigen works

A routing and permission system on a recursive graph

Eigen reads the documents once and keeps them as a graph. Each passage is linked to the passages it shares a name, a date or a subject with; linked passages form a group; groups link to groups; and each node of the graph is itself a graph of what sits beneath it. A question is routed down that graph to the few passages it needs, through a permission check at every level, and the answer comes back with the route it took.

Persistence

Documents are read once and kept as a record of passages: what each says, the names, dates and numbers in it, and its links to other passages. The next question starts from the graph instead of reading everything again.

Permission

An agent reaches the record only through the graph, and opens only the groups its question needs. A file the agent may not read is never opened for it; if a fact it needs sits in one, Eigen delivers that fact alone, transformed as the rights policy for that agent requires, with a name or a figure removed. The owner of the documents sets the policy.

Provenance

Every answer carries the passages it used and a fingerprint of each. Anyone with the sources can check each passage themselves, and if a passage has changed since, the answer is flagged as no longer backed.

Use

Who it is for

Customers

Companies whose AI products answer questions for lawyers, analysts, underwriters, doctors or researchers, where a wrong or unsupported answer is costly. Research groups will be the first users.

How it is delivered

As software the customer runs inside their own systems. Only the text of each request leaves, to the customer's own AI provider.

Who sets the rules

The owner of the documents decides, document by document, which agents may read what. Eigen enforces it on every request.

Compared with today's tools

Today an AI's answer is judged by another AI, which gives a score. Eigen gives, with each answer, the list of passages it rests on, which the customer, or their auditor, can check against the sources.

When

The first pilots are planned for early 2027.

Early prototypes

Eigen as part of an integrated research environment

The first use of Eigen has been as the record underneath a research environment: it holds a project's sources, plans and drafts; papers and talks are written from it. Eigen built the graph for a paper on a quantum-control algorithm it wrote in July 2026. Each definition, claim, result and limitation in the paper is a dot; the lines are the links between them. Hover or tap a dot to read it.

Benchmark

SuperARC

A published test gives a model 100 short sequences of ones and zeros and asks for the rule behind each. Eigen ran every proposed rule before answering with it. 96 of its 100 answers were real rules; most of the models in the published results wrote the sequence back out.
