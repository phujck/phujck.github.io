Why separate expectation from evidence?

Recognising the logarithm in the first paper is easy. Deciding what the construction adds requires asking which assumptions produce it, what the organiser costs, and what changes in the follow-on. In our reading trials, an agent checked the proofs and still allowed a remark about language to steer its final assessment.

The first reply leaves a visible forecast before the model sees the proofs. The second message asks it to read every section and appendix, revisit each consequential objection, and say which result would change if the disputed statement were removed. The same question applies to a favourable first impression. The final explanation is for a colleague deciding whether to read the papers.

Research behind this design
  • In a study of research ideas, changing the prompt to mention other reviewers’ novelty judgements changed some models’ decisions on the very same idea pairs. The effect varied across judges. Sternlicht et al., Old Ideas, Novel Problems ↗
  • Changing only the title style moved review scores for identical generated abstracts. In a separate experiment, signalling that a user liked or disliked a piece of writing shifted the tone of assistants’ feedback. The opening question therefore invites either a surprising or an expected result. Du, TitleTrap ↗ · Sharma et al., Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models ↗
  • Four models gave substantially different editorial recommendations on 200 already-published articles and differed in their ability to detect deliberately planted flaws. That motivates asking what an objection actually invalidates. Le Guellec et al., Major Revision, Minor Accountability ↗
  • A related video study compared model responses across clips when relevant evidence was preserved, altered or removed. It offers an analogy for checking what a judgement depends on. The experiments measured video reasoning. Kang et al., Behavior Pack Optimization for Video MLLM Post-Training ↗

These findings motivate the design. A before-and-after reply lets you inspect the model’s update. Its second answer still sees the first, and this exercise cannot establish that a judgement is unbiased.
