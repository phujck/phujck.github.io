For a prescribed architecture, let B(n)B(n) be its budget, K0=B(1)K_0=B(1) and g(n)=B(n)−K0g(n)=B(n)-K_0. Assume a nonzero refinement price. Suppose its differential cost is scale covariant.

Thus recursive consistency permits nonadditive excess costs as well as the logarithmic case. The geometric refinement of Eq. (34) realises this family on the ranges N=4kN=4^k, with α=1−γ/d\alpha=1-\gamma/d, and there Eq. (D8) reduces to Eq. (35). Positive α\alpha makes a fixed refinement more expensive on a larger register, and negative α\alpha makes it cheaper and gives a bounded limiting cost. Physically, unlimited distinctions ought to require an unlimited budget, which would exclude the negative-α\alpha members of this family. The ordinary logarithm is selected by the specific condition α=0\alpha=0, for which the refinement price is independent of the existing register size.

The geometric picture produced this family from one assumption about how a price depends on size, but the power law in Eq. (D1) need not be tied to geometry, nor postulated. Suppose only that the positive prices of the same refinement at two initial ranges differ by a factor depending on the ratio of those ranges, Δqm(n)=h(q)Δm(n)\Delta_{qm}(n)=h(q)\Delta_m(n), and that this factor varies monotonically with qq. Applying the relation twice gives h(qq′)=h(q)h(q′)h(qq')=h(q)h(q'), and the argument of Theorem 2, applied to logh\log h, gives h(q)=qαh(q)=q^\alpha. A refinement price with no preferred scale is therefore necessarily scale covariant, and homogeneous refinement is the case in which the price does not depend on scale at all.
