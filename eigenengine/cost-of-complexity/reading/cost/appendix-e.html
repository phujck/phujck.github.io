Appendix E — Monte Carlo test of the perimeter readout

The readout of Eq. (39) follows from counting walls. A point placed uniformly in a region of area AA and moved a short distance ss in a uniformly random direction crosses a given straight wall element of length ℓ\ell from a given side with probability sℓ/(πA)s\ell/(\pi A), to leading order in ss. Summing over the walls at depth kk, each crossable from both of the cells it separates, the probability that the address at depth kk changes is sCk/(πA)sC_k/(\pi A), with CkC_k the summed perimeters of the cells at that depth. Every wall at depth kk is also a wall at depth k+1k+1, so the conditional probability is Ck/Ck+1C_k/C_{k+1} whatever the shapes of the cells. Within a single parent the same count applies, with its outline crossed from inside only and its internal walls from either side.

On the hexagonal lattice the identity holds exactly rather than to leading order. Three cells meet at every vertex, so a step shorter than a cell side ends either in its own cell or in one of the six that share an edge with it, and by the symmetry of the lattice these six are equally likely. Every exposed cell edge then carries the same weight, and the probability that a step changes an address is proportional to the number of exposed edges separating different addresses. On the square lattice, where four cells meet at a vertex, a step can end in a cell that shares only a corner with its start, and this equality fails.

We tested Eq. (39) on regions of hexagonal cells of unit pitch, with points placed uniformly in a parent and moved by isotropic steps of length log-uniform between 0.060.06 and 0.280.28 pitches, and with perimeters counted as exposed cell edges. The cases were the Gosper hierarchy with children of 7m7^m cells for m=0,…,4m=0,\dots,4, the same hierarchy read across two and three levels at once, a Gosper parent of 343343 cells divided into 22, 33, 55, 99, 1717 and 3333 irregular children grown from random seeds, and a hexagonal parent of 217217 cells divided into 33, 77 and 1919 such children. Each case used between 1.0×1061.0\times10^{6} and 1.5×1071.5\times10^{7} steps, giving between 6.0×1046.0\times10^{4} and 4.9×1054.9\times10^{5} changes of the finer address. Figure 8 compares the measured conditional probability with the counted perimeter ratio. All sixteen cases agree within 1.91.9 binomial standard errors, with χ2=11.4\chi^2=11.4 for sixteen degrees of freedom. No physical material or heat is measured, so the test confirms only the counting that links address statistics to perimeter.
