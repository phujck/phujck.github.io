Appendix C — Cost of a symbol

Theorem 4 compares two ways of paying for a symbol. Its first part rests on the observation that the rr most typical symbols of any representation already form an organisation of rr distinctions, so no symbol can cost less than the efficient register for its own typicality prefix. The bound therefore includes the structural cost of reaching a symbol, not only the cost within its final register. The second part supplies a construction that approaches this bound. A variable-length description must record where each word ends, and a terminator makes that record explicit. The length can instead be marked recursively, by writing the number of digits before the digits themselves, which adds only O(loglogr)O(\log\log r) overhead and attains the same leading rate [73, 74]. The leading coefficient is fixed by the register comparison, while the lower-order overhead records how a particular code identifies its words and decides the behaviour at the boundary of an infinite vocabulary (Section V). Neither part suffices alone, since a lower bound leaves open whether any representation approaches it, while an explicit encoding cannot exclude a better one. Together they identify the same κ∞\kappa_\infty for the efficient register budget and for the infimum of achievable symbol-cost rates.

Proof. For (i), the rr most typical symbols are realised by ℛ\mathcal R at a largest cost of fℛ(r)f_{\mathcal R}(r), since fℛf_{\mathcal R} is nondecreasing. As in the proof of Theorem 1, an efficient register for rr distinctions cannot need a larger budget than an available alternative, so Kreg(r)≤fℛ(r)K_{\mathrm{reg}}(r)\le f_{\mathcal R}(r), and the lower bound of Eq. (28) completes the chain. For (ii), use an alphabet of bb letters together with one terminator, each held in a digit of range b+1b+1. There are blb^l words of ll letters, so at least bLb^L words have at most LL letters. Assign ranks in order of increasing length. The symbol at rank rr then has at most ⌈logbr⌉≤logbr+1\lceil\log_b r\rceil\le\log_b r+1 letters and, with its terminator, occupies at most logbr+2\log_b r+2 digits, each of budget Kreg(b+1)K_{\mathrm{reg}}(b+1). Its cost is nondecreasing in rank, which gives Eq. (30). The rate of this organisation is Kreg(b+1)/logbK_{\mathrm{reg}}(b+1)/\log b, which tends to κ∞\kappa_\infty as b→∞b\to\infty by Eq. (27), while by (i) no organisation has a rate below κ∞\kappa_\infty.
