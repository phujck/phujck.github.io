Physical origins of the Kalmár constant · Gerard McCaul

I. Introduction

Reference [1] assigns a cost to the organisation of a representation as well as to its symbols. Its starting observation is that a register holds distinctions at one level and is itself selected by a distinction at the next. With additive positional composition, the efficient register budget Kreg(N) grows logarithmically in the number N of distinctions supported, and under homogeneous refinement Kreg(N) = K₀ + κ ln N.

The quantity Kreg is defined as an infimum over a class 𝒜N of admissible organisations. Every selector inside an organisation is priced. The selection of the organisation from 𝒜N is not. It is made by whoever takes the infimum. For a designed register this is appropriate. For a system that revises its own architecture [2, 3], the choice of organisation is a distinction that the system must hold, and Ref. [1] states that its accounting admits no exceptions.

What does it cost to hold that choice? The answer depends on how many organisations there are. For the positional organisations used throughout Ref. [1] the count is a classical arithmetic function, the number H(N) of ordered factorisations of N [4–6]. Its growth is governed by Kalmár’s constant ρ = 1.7286 …, the solution of ζ(ρ) = 2.

We obtain four results. Section IV shows that the number of organisations of budget at most B grows as exp(λc B/κ), with λc fixed by the existence cost through ζ(λc) = 1 + exp(λc K₀/κ). Section V gives the maximum-entropy ensemble of organisations and its critical line. Section VI prices a register that holds its own organisation and gives the condition under which that account closes. Section VII iterates the construction and resums the resulting hierarchy. Section II recalls what is needed from Ref. [1], and Section III the number theory. Proofs are collected in the appendices.
