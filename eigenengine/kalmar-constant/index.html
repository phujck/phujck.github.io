Sixteen symbols have eight layouts. What does it cost to hold the chosen layout?

The Cost of Complexity prices the choices inside a layout. This unreviewed draft charges for the layout itself.

Read the draft PDF

Same sixteen symbols. Eight layouts.

A register holds the alternatives for one choice. In 2 × 8, choose one of two registers, then one of eight symbols. In 8 × 2, reverse those group sizes. Symbol 11 keeps its rank as it moves.

Choose a layout

Choose boundaries in symbol 11’s address

1 0 10

The 11th symbol has zero-based address 10, written as 1010 in binary. Each of the three gaps can hold a register boundary, giving 23 = 8 layouts.

Figure 1, p. 3 ↗

Count every layout up to a limit.

Let H(N) count the layouts for exactly N symbols. For sixteen, H(16) = 8. Kalmár’s constant describes the growth of a different count, the total across all sizes up to N.

If the first choice has n options, the remaining choices can cover at most N/n positions. Every nonempty layout starts on one of these branches.

For a total growing as Nρ, the fraction starting with n approaches n−ρ. The branch fractions sum to one. The zeta sum adds the term for 1.

2−ρ + 3−ρ + 4−ρ + … = 1 ⇒ ζ(ρ) = 2

ρ ≈ 1.72865

Doubling the allowed range gives about 3.31 times as many layouts in total, for large ranges.

Kalmár’s theorem, Eq. (12), p. 2 ↗

Charging for each register slows the growth.

Now count layouts by cost. A register with n choices costs K0 + κ ln n. The constant κ converts the logarithm into a budget. The fixed charge K0 is paid for every register in the layout.

Choose a fixed cost

No fixed charge

λc = 1.72865

Without a fixed charge, the budget count gives the same exponent as Kalmár’s range count.

ζ(λc) = 1 + exp(λc K0/κ)

At large budget B, each additional κ of budget multiplies the count by about eλc. As the fixed charge rises, λc decreases toward 1, the convergence boundary of the infinite-range Zipf distribution.

Four bits for the symbols. Ten to hold their layout too.

Three bits say where the register boundaries go. That three-bit record has a layout of its own. Keep recording until one bit is left, with no gap to describe.

  1. Choose a symbol16 possibilities4 bits
  2. Record that layout8 possibilities3 bits
  3. Record the three-bit layout4 possibilities2 bits
  4. Record the two-bit layout2 possibilities1 bit
4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 10 bits

48 never gets smaller.

48⟳48

With 48 symbols there are 48 layouts. Recording that choice leads back to the same size.

817 of 9,999 sizes tested from 2 to 10,000 fail to finish. Of those, 811 reach a fixed size and six enter a two-size cycle.

What if the records of layouts also need a layout?

The ten-bit record has four constituent registers. The draft’s next level stores how those registers are grouped, then applies the same rule to the new record. On a four-bit payload, the successive budgets are

How many levels would a layout typically use?

With only the average cost specified, the maximum-entropy distribution assigns a geometric distribution to the number of levels. The average depth grows without bound as the weighting parameter approaches λc from above. The draft proposes a corresponding pattern of branch sizes in self-organising hierarchies. That prediction awaits a data test.

Which claims belong to the draft?

The ordered-factorisation count and Kalmár’s theorem are classical. The draft interprets the constant as a capacity under a stated cost law. Its literature search for that interpretation, the threshold and the closure results was limited. The model prices holding a layout whose subregisters at each level share a size. Changing a layout would need a separate account.
